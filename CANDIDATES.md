# Candidate shortlist — Two-Sentence Horror

Picked by the judge brief, in order: (1) genuinely frightening in exactly
two sentences, (2) second sentence turns the first, (3) horror implied not
explained, (4) precise concrete prose, (5) original.

## Chosen (271 chars, 2 sentences)

> My therapist told me to keep a dream journal, and for a month every entry has been the same: a man with my face standing at the foot of my bed, smiling. Last night I woke up holding the pen, and the newest entry, in my handwriting, reads: ‘Tonight I finally get to stay.’

Strongest on criteria 2 and 3: the narrator himself is the author of the
entries, so the double is no longer visiting his dreams — he is taking over
the waking world, one entry at a time. "Get to stay" is the whole threat,
never spelled out.

## Rejected

- **The watch (155 chars).** "My late husband's watch still ticks on my
  wrist, though it stopped the day he died. This morning it ticked
  backwards, and the date on its face is tomorrow." Elegant and restrained,
  but the scare is a single image (tomorrow = death) with no re-read turn;
  the second sentence does not make the first worse, it just adds a fact.
- **The photos (229 chars).** "Every photo I take of my daughter shows a
  second child standing just behind her... She told me this morning that
  the girl in the photos is her, and that the one standing here is the one
  who got out." Good identity flip, but "the real one was replaced" is a
  well-worn twist and the scare lands softer than the dream journal's.
- **The letters (209 chars, 3 sentences).** "The grief counsellor said
  writing letters to my dead wife would help... This morning there was a
  reply in her handwriting: 'Stop writing. He reads them first.'" Rejected
  on form: the quoted "Stop writing." reads as a third sentence, which
  risks the judge's first criterion (exactly two sentences).
- **Under the bed (258 chars).** "I teach my daughter to check under the
  bed every night... 'Mummy, the man under the bed says you stopped
  checking years ago, and he's been waiting ever since.'" Solid turn, but
  "the man under the bed" is one of the most familiar horror images there
  is; it loses on originality (criterion 5).
